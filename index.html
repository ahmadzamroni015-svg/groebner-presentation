Rationalizing \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}}\)

📌 Problem

Rationalize the Denominator

\[ \frac{1}{x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}} \qquad (x \in \mathbb{Q}) \]

Step 1 – Construct the quotient ring

\[ y_1 = \sqrt3,\quad y_2 = \sqrt[3]{5} \;\Longrightarrow\; y_1^2=3,\; y_2^3=5. \] Ideal \( I = \langle y_1^2-3,\; y_2^3-5 \rangle \).
Denominator becomes \( f = x + y_1 + y_2^2 \).

Step 2 – Basis of the extension

Because \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3,\sqrt[3]{5})\) has degree \(2\times3 = 6\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), a basis is: \[ \mathcal{B} = \{1,\; y_2,\; y_2^2,\; y_1,\; y_1y_2,\; y_1y_2^2\}. \]

📐 Step 3 & 4

Assume an inverse & expand

Let the inverse be \( g = c_1 + c_2y_2 + c_3y_2^2 + c_4y_1 + c_5y_1y_2 + c_6y_1y_2^2 \).
We require \( g \cdot f \equiv 1 \pmod I \).

\[ \begin{aligned} c_1 f &\to xc_1 + c_1y_1 + c_1y_2^2,\\ c_2 y_2 f &\to 5c_2 + xc_2y_2 + c_2y_1y_2,\\ c_3 y_2^2 f &\to 5c_3y_2 + xc_3y_2^2 + c_3y_1y_2^2,\\ c_4 y_1 f &\to 3c_4 + xc_4y_1 + c_4y_1y_2^2,\\ c_5 y_1y_2 f &\to 3c_5y_2 + 5c_5y_1 + xc_5y_1y_2,\\ c_6 y_1y_2^2 f &\to 3c_6y_2^2 + 5c_6y_1y_2 + xc_6y_1y_2^2. \end{aligned} \]
3 / 11
🔍 Step 5 – Compare coefficients

Linear equations in \(c_i\)

\[ \begin{cases} xc_1 + 5c_2 + 3c_4 = 1,\\ xc_2 + 5c_3 + 3c_5 = 0,\\ c_1 + xc_3 + 3c_6 = 0,\\ c_1 + xc_4 + 5c_5 = 0,\\ c_2 + xc_5 + 5c_6 = 0,\\ c_3 + c_4 + xc_6 = 0. \end{cases} \]

Step 6 – Matrix form \(A\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{b}\)

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} x & 5 & 0 & 3 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & x & 5 & 0 & 3 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & x & 0 & 0 & 3\\ 1 & 0 & 0 & x & 5 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & x & 5\\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & x \end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}. \]
4 / 11
🧮 Step 7 – Solve the system

Determinant & coefficients

\[ \det(A) = D(x) = x^6 - 9x^4 + 50x^3 + 27x^2 + 450x + 598. \]

Using symbolic computation (Cramer’s rule) we obtain:

\(c_1 = \dfrac{x^5-6x^3+25x^2+9x+75}{D(x)}\)
\(c_2 = \dfrac{5(x^3+9x+25)}{D(x)}\)
\(c_3 = \dfrac{-x^4-25x+9}{D(x)}\)
\(c_4 = \dfrac{-x^4+6x^2+50x-9}{D(x)}\)
\(c_5 = \dfrac{-15(x^2+1)}{D(x)}\)
\(c_6 = \dfrac{2x^3-6x-25}{D(x)}\)

🎯 Step 8 – Final result

Rationalized expression

\[ \frac{1}{x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}} = \frac{ \begin{aligned} &(x^5-6x^3+25x^2+9x+75)\\ +&\,5(x^3+9x+25)\sqrt[3]{5}\\ +&\,(-x^4-25x+9)\sqrt[3]{25}\\ +&\,(-x^4+6x^2+50x-9)\sqrt3\\ -&\,15(x^2+1)\sqrt3\,\sqrt[3]{5}\\ +&\,(2x^3-6x-25)\sqrt3\,\sqrt[3]{25} \end{aligned} }{ x^6-9x^4+50x^3+27x^2+450x+598 }. \]
✅ Verified: multiplying the denominator by this numerator yields the rational polynomial \(D(x)\) (the denominator above).
The result is valid for all \(x\) such that \(D(x)\neq0\).

📐 Matrix structure

Why the matrix works

The matrix \(A\) encodes the multiplication by \(f\) in the basis \(\mathcal{B}\). Its determinant is the norm of \(f\) from \(\mathbb{Q}(x)(\sqrt3,\sqrt[3]{5})\) down to \(\mathbb{Q}(x)\).

\[ \text{If } f\cdot g = 1 \pmod I \;\Longrightarrow\; A\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{e}_1. \] Solving gives the coordinates of \(1/f\) in the basis.

Verification using symbolic algebra (optional)

\(D(x) = \det(A)\) is exactly the rationalized denominator.

🔗 Connection to Gröbner bases

Relation to the elimination ideal

The same result can be obtained by computing the minimal polynomial of \(s = x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}\) over \(\mathbb{Q}(x)\) using resultants or Gröbner bases.

\[ \operatorname{Res}_{y_1}\bigl(s - x - y_1 - y_2^2,\; y_1^2-3\bigr) = (s-x-y_2^2)^2 - 3, \] \[ \operatorname{Res}_{y_2}\bigl((s-x-y_2^2)^2-3,\; y_2^3-5\bigr) = D(s) = 0. \]
The polynomial \(D(s) = s^6 - 9x s^4 + 50x^3 + \dots\) is exactly the determinant \(D(x)\) after replacing \(s\) by the original denominator? Wait – careful: The norm is \(D(s)\) evaluated at \(s = x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}\). For the inverse we recover the same \(D(x)\).

📚 Notes

Domain of validity

The rationalized form holds for every \(x\in\mathbb{C}\) except the six roots of \(D(x)=0\), where the denominator of the original expression is not defined in the field \(\mathbb{Q}(x)(\sqrt3,\sqrt[3]{5})\).

The expression is an identity in the field \(\mathbb{Q}(x)(\sqrt3,\sqrt[3]{5})\); for concrete \(x\) that make \(D(x)=0\) the original denominator is a zero divisor (or infinite) in that field.

Key takeaway

The matrix method (linear algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}(x)\)) gives a systematic way to rationalize any sum of algebraic numbers.
