Exercise 2.1.7 – Rationalising the denominator

\[ \frac{1}{x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}} \qquad (x\in\mathbb{Q}) \]

We present a complete, step‑by‑step solution using the quotient ring method and linear algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}(x)\).

Step 1

Construct the quotient ring

Let \(y_1 = \sqrt{3}\) and \(y_2 = \sqrt[3]{5}\). Then

\[ y_1^2 = 3,\qquad y_2^3 = 5. \]

Define the ideal

\[ I = \langle y_1^2-3,\; y_2^3-5\rangle \;\subset\; \mathbb{Q}(x)[y_1,y_2]. \]

The denominator becomes

\[ f = x + y_1 + y_2^2. \]
Step 2

Determine a basis of the quotient

Because \(y_1^2 \equiv 3\) and \(y_2^3 \equiv 5\) modulo \(I\), every polynomial reduces to a linear combination of

\[ \mathcal{B} = \bigl\{1,\; y_2,\; y_2^2,\; y_1,\; y_1y_2,\; y_1y_2^2\bigr\}. \]

Hence the quotient space \(\mathbb{Q}(x)[y_1,y_2]/I\) has dimension \(2\times 3 = 6\) over \(\mathbb{Q}(x)\).

Step 3

Assume an inverse

We look for \(g\) of the form

\[ g = c_1 + c_2 y_2 + c_3 y_2^2 + c_4 y_1 + c_5 y_1 y_2 + c_6 y_1 y_2^2, \]

with \(c_i \in \mathbb{Q}(x)\), such that \(g\cdot f \equiv 1 \pmod I\).

Step 4

Expand and reduce \(g\cdot f\)

Using \(f = x + y_1 + y_2^2\) and the reduction rules \(y_1^2 \to 3\), \(y_2^3 \to 5\), we obtain:

\[ \begin{aligned} c_1(1\cdot f) &\longrightarrow xc_1 + c_1y_1 + c_1y_2^2,\\ c_2(y_2\cdot f) &\longrightarrow 5c_2 + xc_2y_2 + c_2y_1y_2,\\ c_3(y_2^2\cdot f) &\longrightarrow 5c_3y_2 + xc_3y_2^2 + c_3y_1y_2^2,\\ c_4(y_1\cdot f) &\longrightarrow 3c_4 + xc_4y_1 + c_4y_1y_2^2,\\ c_5(y_1y_2\cdot f) &\longrightarrow 3c_5y_2 + 5c_5y_1 + xc_5y_1y_2,\\ c_6(y_1y_2^2\cdot f) &\longrightarrow 3c_6y_2^2 + 5c_6y_1y_2 + xc_6y_1y_2^2. \end{aligned} \]
Step 5

Compare coefficients of the basis \(\mathcal{B}\)

Collecting the coefficients of \(1, y_2, y_2^2, y_1, y_1y_2, y_1y_2^2\) and setting the result equal to \(1\) (the constant term 1, all other basis coefficients 0) gives the linear system:

\[ \begin{cases} xc_1 + 5c_2 + 3c_4 = 1,\\[2pt] xc_2 + 5c_3 + 3c_5 = 0,\\[2pt] c_1 + xc_3 + 3c_6 = 0,\\[2pt] c_1 + xc_4 + 5c_5 = 0,\\[2pt] c_2 + xc_5 + 5c_6 = 0,\\[2pt] c_3 + c_4 + xc_6 = 0. \end{cases} \]
Step 6

Matrix form \(A\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}\)

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} x & 5 & 0 & 3 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & x & 5 & 0 & 3 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & x & 0 & 0 & 3\\ 1 & 0 & 0 & x & 5 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & x & 5\\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & x \end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf{c} = \begin{pmatrix}c_1\\c_2\\c_3\\c_4\\c_5\\c_6\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}. \]
Step 7

Solve the linear system (symbolic computation)

The determinant of \(A\) is

\[ D(x)=\det(A)=x^6-9x^4+50x^3+27x^2+450x+598. \]

Solving (e.g., by Cramer’s rule) yields the coefficients:

\[ \begin{aligned} c_1 &= \frac{x^5-6x^3+25x^2+9x+75}{D(x)},\\[4pt] c_2 &= \frac{5(x^3+9x+25)}{D(x)},\\[4pt] c_3 &= \frac{-x^4-25x+9}{D(x)},\\[4pt] c_4 &= \frac{-x^4+6x^2+50x-9}{D(x)},\\[4pt] c_5 &= \frac{-15(x^2+1)}{D(x)},\\[4pt] c_6 &= \frac{2x^3-6x-25}{D(x)}. \end{aligned} \]
Step 8

Final rationalised form

Substitute back \(y_1 = \sqrt3\) and \(y_2 = \sqrt[3]{5}\) (so \(y_2^2 = \sqrt[3]{25}\)):

\[ \boxed{ \frac{1}{x+\sqrt3+\sqrt[3]{25}} = \frac{ \begin{array}{l} \bigl(x^5-6x^3+25x^2+9x+75\bigr) \\ +\;5\bigl(x^3+9x+25\bigr)\sqrt[3]{5} \\ +\;\bigl(-x^4-25x+9\bigr)\sqrt[3]{25} \\ +\;\bigl(-x^4+6x^2+50x-9\bigr)\sqrt3 \\ -\;15(x^2+1)\,\sqrt3\,\sqrt[3]{5} \\ +\;\bigl(2x^3-6x-25\bigr)\,\sqrt3\,\sqrt[3]{25} \end{array} }{ x^6-9x^4+50x^3+27x^2+450x+598 }. } \]
